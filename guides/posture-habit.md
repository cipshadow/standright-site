# Build a posture habit in a few seconds a day

{% include updated.html %}

A posture habit on Android can stay private. You answer a check-in yourself, and the answers stay on the phone. Remembering to "sit up" all day is a goal. A habit is a cue you already meet, one small answer, and a record of what you answered.

## A situation, not a clock

[Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts, and Wardle (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) asked 96 volunteers to repeat a new eating, drinking, or activity behavior in the same situation each day for 84 days. They chose situations rather than clock times as the cue, because a situation can prompt the action while a time has to be watched for. The paper is about those everyday behaviors. It is not a result about phones, and it is not a forecast that a check-in app will make anything automatic.

Use it for the cue. Pick a moment that already happens, and keep the action small enough to do when that moment arrives. Unlocking the phone is one such moment. How often it actually happens is in [Can I use unlocking my phone as a cue for a new habit?](/guides/phone-unlock-as-a-habit-cue/) This page stays with the shape of the habit.

## One answer, and it is yours

The action is one tap: upright, or slouched. That tap is a report you make. It is not a reading of your body. "Slouched" is a line in the log. It is not a score, and it is not a miss you are supposed to outgrow.

## The record is the log

A calendar of your own check-ins shows what you tapped, over weeks. It does not show a measurement of how you sat. A day with no answers is a day with no answers.

## Hours

The cue should be absent when you do not want it. Choose the days and the hours, leave a gap between checks, and keep the last part of the day quiet. A pause you can start with one tap belongs with those settings.
