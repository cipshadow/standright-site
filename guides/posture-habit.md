# Build a posture habit in a few seconds a day

Sitting up straight is easy for a minute and hard to remember for a day. That's why posture works better as a habit than as a goal. A habit needs three things: a cue you can't miss, a tiny action, and a way to see that you're keeping it up.

## 1. Tie it to a cue you already have

The best cue is something you already do many times a day without thinking. Unlocking your phone is ideal: it happens often, it's often exactly when you start to slouch, and it needs no new routine. Each unlock becomes a moment to ask yourself one question: am I sitting up?

## 2. Make the action tiny

The action should take seconds. Lift your head, roll your shoulders back, bring the phone up. That's it. No exercises, no timer. Small actions repeated often build the habit faster than a long session you skip.

## 3. Answer honestly, without guilt

Saying "slouched" isn't a failure. It's the moment you noticed, and noticing is the habit you're building. Over time you'll catch yourself before the check-in does.

## 4. See it build

Seeing your progress keeps you going. A simple calendar or streak is enough. Look for a trend over weeks, not a perfect day.

## 5. Give it quiet hours

A habit that nags at the wrong time gets switched off. Pick the days and hours that suit you, and let the evening wind down without prompts.
